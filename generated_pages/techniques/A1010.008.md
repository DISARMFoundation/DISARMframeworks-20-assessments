# Assessment A1010.008: Subject Develops Informational Learned Helplessness

**Summary**: Summary: Activity has been assessed to have had the impact of inducing Informational Learned Helplessness, which is a state in which an individual believes they're unable to discern what information is true and what information is false, comes to accept this state as an unchangeable norm, and no longer attempts to tell the two apart.<br><br>Detail: In a paper titled “[Russian News Media, Digital Media, Informational Learned Helplessness, and Belief in COVID-19 Misinformation](https://academic.oup.com/ijpor/article/33/3/571/6294447)”, researchers Erik Nisbet and Olga Kamenchuk introduced the concept of Informational Learned Helplessness (ILH).<br><br>ILH is based on psychological research in which individuals who experience prolonged exposure to difficult or uncontrollable situations which they cannot avoid or remedy come to accept the situation as a given in what is called “learned helplessness”. This leads to the individual no longer attempting to correct or avoid the situation, as they believe there is no way their actions can alter the outcomes they experience.<br><br>Nisbet and Kamenchuk argue that “the high volume of competing claims and information inherent to infodemics increases cognitive and emotional costs of discerning accurate from inaccurate information for audiences”, which leads to ILH; an individual’s belief that they are unable to discern what information is true and what information is false, and their acceptance of this state as an unchangeable norm.
