# Assessment A1012.005: Subject Dies by Suicide

**Summary**: Summary: Activity has the assessed impact of contributing to the subject's death by suicide.<br><br>Detail: This impact records that the subject died by suicide, and that an analyst assesses the activity to have contributed to that outcome.<br><br>One example of activity which brings about death by suicide is sextortion, in which an actor obtains a subject's intimate imagery and then demands payment under threat of distribution. Cases have been documented where the gap between first contact and the target's death by suicide is just a few hours. Sustained harassment campaigns produce the same outcome over much longer periods.<br><br>Example<br><br>Jordan DeMay, a seventeen-year-old in Michigan, was contacted on Instagram in March 2022 by an account posing as a young woman. The account was a hacked Instagram profile purchased by Samuel and Samson Ogoshi. [They obtained an intimate image from him, demanded 1,000 US dollars, and threatened to publish it.](https://www.washingtonpost.com/nation/2024/09/06/sextortion-samuel-samson-ogoshi-jordan-demay/) He died by suicide hours later. [The actors pleaded guilty in April 2024 to conspiracy to sexually exploit minors, and were each sentenced to seventeen and a half years in prison.](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/two-nigerians-sentenced-years-sextortion-case-led-michigan-teens-rcna169730)<br><br>The case demonstrates an organised group of actors using purchased hacked accounts bringing about an impact not part of their original objectives; the suicide of their target.<br><br>Hana Kimura, a 22-year-old Japanese professional wrestler and cast member of the reality series Terrace House, was found dead on 23 May 2020. [Notes left at her home cited the volume of abusive messages she had been receiving on social media,](https://unfpa.org/node/42344) which followed an on-screen argument with another cast member after he had accidentally ruined one of her wrestling costumes while doing his own laundry. [Two men who had posted insults about her online were fined 9,000 yen each,](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/japan-online-insults-jail-cyberbullying-reality-star-death-rcna33669) close to the maximum then available under Japan's insult law, which capped penalties at under 30 days' detention or a fine of under 10,000 yen. Her mother, Kyoko Kimura, campaigned on the inadequacy of those penalties, and on 13 June 2022 Japan's Diet [passed a law raising the maximum punishment for insult to one year's imprisonment or a fine of 300,000 yen,](https://edition.cnn.com/2022/06/14/asia/japan-cyberbullying-law-intl-hnk-scli) with a provision requiring the law to be reviewed within three years of taking effect for its impact on freedom of expression.
