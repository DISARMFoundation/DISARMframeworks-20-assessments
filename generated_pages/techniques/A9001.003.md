# Assessment A9001.003: Permanent Impact

**Summary**: Summary: Impact has been assessed to have continued indefinitely, or to have altered the subject's circumstances permanently.<br><br>Detail: This value records an impact which cannot be undone. The change is settled, and the subject, situation, or audience will not return to the state it held before the activity.<br><br>Some outcomes are irreversible by their nature. A death, an injury which does not heal, or the publication of private material cannot be taken back, so the value follows from what the outcome is rather than from a forecast about what happens next. Others become irreversible through time. Years spent out of a profession and the income which went with them are not recoverable, whatever formal position is reached later. Assess against what has been lost rather than against what remains theoretically possible.<br><br>Where the impact has not been confirmed to be irreversible, use A9001.002: Lasting Impact instead, however long the impact has run so far. An impact which has held for years and can still be undone is lasting rather than permanent.<br><br>Example<br><br>From August 2017 Myanmar security forces conducted a campaign against the Rohingya in Rakhine State. They unlawfully killed thousands of Rohingya, including young children; raped and committed other sexual violence against Rohingya women and girls; tortured Rohingya men and boys in detention sites; and burned down hundreds of Rohingya villages. During this period more than 700,000 people fled to Bangladesh. Amnesty International's September 2022 report The Social Atrocity found that [Meta's engagement-based algorithms proactively amplified content inciting violence and discrimination against the Rohingya in the years before the violence](https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/asa16/5933/2022/en/), and recorded that in mid-2014 the company had one Burmese-speaking content moderator, based in Dublin, for 1.2 million users in Myanmar. As the ninth anniversary of the exodus approached, [around 1.2 million Rohingya refugees were living in Bangladesh in June 2026, most of them in camps in Cox's Bazar.](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/06/1167623)<br><br>This case demonstrates an impact which is irreversible on several counts. The deaths cannot be undone, and nearly nine years of displacement cannot be returned even if repatriation eventually proceeds. The permanency does not rest on predicting that return will never happen; rather on the time already lost.
