# Assessment A3004.002: Harass en Masse

**Summary**: <b>Summary:</b><br>Harass en Masse describes more than one asset or individual acting on one target close enough together that the target receives what they send as a mass rather than as a sequence. What identifies the Technique is not who built the mass but that a mass arrived. Examples include a raid organised on a private channel with a target list and shared tooling, an audience acting on a name its amplifier gave them, and a crowd that converges on a target with nobody having organised anything. In Harass en Masse the volume is part of the harm; behaviour which may otherwise be brushed off can be overwhelming when received in plurality over a short period of time.<br><br><b>Detail:</b><br>Harass en Masse is one form of harassment, and an incident has to satisfy four conditions before it is recorded as this Technique.<br><br><b>Targeting:</b> There was a specific target of harassment (a person, a group, an institution), and a participant acted on that target. Identifying the target is a precondition for applying the Technique; for harassment to occur, a target must have been harassed.<br><br><b>Intent to harass, or reasonable understanding of the culmination of behaviour as harassment:</b> Either the actor sought to alarm, distress, frighten, or constrain the target, or a reasonable person knowing what the actor knew, and taking into consideration the position of the target, would have understood the conduct to do so. For more information about both the "intent to harass" and "reasonable understanding of behaviour as harassment" paths, see <b>A3004.001: Harass</b>.<br><br>Note that when looking at behaviour conducted as part of mass harassment, the bar for what is considered to be harassing is lower, as the mass of abuse amplifies the harm experienced. Where one message alone may not be considered harassment, when seen as part of a wave of abuse, it can hit the bar for being considered harassment.<br><br><b>Plurality:</b> More than one asset or individual acted on the target. For example, a group of individuals collaborating to harass a target, or a single actor using automation to send many bot accounts at a target.<br><br><b>Convergence in time:</b> The contributions arrived close enough together that the target received them as a mass rather than as a sequence. The comparison is against what the target could reasonably have dealt with, not against a fixed number or a rule of thumb about the channel: a streamer with a moderation team can absorb a rush of messages that would overwhelm one without, and the same message rate is routine in one chat and unmanageable in another.<br><br>Coordination of the harassment is not a condition of this Technique. Whether an actor manufactured mass harassment is a judgement about actors rather than about what happened to the target, and it is recorded separately. The reason is that an analyst is much more likely to be able to confirm that harassment en masse has occurred than to confirm that it was explicitly coordinated. Separating coordination from this Technique lets analysts document harassment en masse without also confirming whether it was coordinated.<br><br><b>Different Ways a Mass Forms:</b><br>A mass forms four ways. All four are in scope, and which one applies is recorded as actor analysis rather than as a condition of the Technique.<br><br><b>Group agreement.</b> Actors within an existing group arrange the harassment campaign between themselves. There is a channel, a list, a piece of tooling, or a thread where the target is posted, and the group collaborates to harass the target.<br><br>Meta describes this behaviour as "brigading", defining it as [people working together to mass comment, mass post, or engage in other repetitive mass behaviours to harass or silence others](https://about.fb.com/news/2021/12/metas-adversarial-threat-report/). In their example, a network it removed, linked to the anti-vaccination movement V_V in Italy and France targeted medical professionals, journalists, and elected officials, and Graphika described the group as [using Telegram to train recruits and to circulate word of who to target](https://www.graphika.com/reports/viral-vendetta).<br><br><b>Actors controlling multiple assets.</b> One operator provides the plurality using technology such as automation. A single actor, or several coordinating actors who each control multiple assets, deploy a harassment campaign which appears to be a vast wave of harassment coming from a wider pool of collaborators than actually originated the attack.<br><br>For example, Twitch alleged in its September 2021 complaint that [one defendant alone was linked to around 3,000 bot accounts deployed in hate raids](https://techcrunch.com/2021/09/13/twitch-hate-raids-lawsuits/), and that the defendants altered their code to evade detection.<br><br><b>Recruitment.</b> An actor assembles people who need not know each other, by encouraging an audience to act on a target. For example, the pattern in which a user [posts a link to a YouTube video on 4chan's /pol/, actors arrive together, and raiders return afterwards to report what they did](https://seclab.bu.edu/people/gianluca/papers/youtube-CSCW2019.pdf). There is no membership and no list, only a thread and a convention.<br><br>An actor who names a target but does not explicitly ask their followers to harass them is explored further in the Technique <b>A3004.004: Identify a Target to an Audience Likely to Act</b>.<br><br><b>Emergence.</b> Nobody assembles the mass. Researchers named mass harassment arising without an organiser as an [online firestorm](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13527266.2013.797778), a sudden discharge of large quantities of negative messages against a person, company, or group, often expressing indignation without pointing to a specific criticism. Nothing in that description requires an organiser, and in a firestorm there usually is not one.<br><br><b>Harassing en Masse as a Method of Information Suppression:</b><br>The V_V network is the case where mass harassment is deployed with the goal of suppressing unwanted information. Graphika described the group as [using Telegram to train recruits and to circulate word of who to target](https://www.graphika.com/reports/viral-vendetta), directing them at medical professionals, journalists, and elected officials, providing direct links to their posts with templated harassment ready to paste in. Their targets' profiles were flooded with harassment including swastikas, and accusations of genocide, overwhelming the conversation and dissuading users from sharing accurate public health information.<br><br><b>Potential Impacts:</b><br>Impacts are organised by affected party. An item may appear under more than one party where the justification differs.<br><br><b>Primary Target:</b><br><b>A1010.005: Subject Ceases Sharing Information.</b> Twitch pleaded that some streamers stopped broadcasting until an attack ended.<br><b>A1010.006: Subject Self-Censors when Sharing Information.</b> Targets avoid the subjects that drew a mob, which is cheaper than absorbing another one.<br><b>A1010.007: Subject's Audience Disengages with Information Shared by Subject.</b> A chat or reply thread filled faster than it can be read displaces the target's own community along with the abuse.<br><b>A1011.002: Activity Restricts Subject's Freedom of Expression.</b> Being unable to be heard through the volume is a restriction achieved without any prohibition.<br><b>A1008.006: Feeling of Anxiety Evoked in Subject.</b> [Cai and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16248) record psychological harm as one of the harms hate raids cause marginalised streamers.<br><b>A1008.017: Feeling of Fear Evoked in Subject.</b> They record safety threat as another, since a mob contains contributions a single harasser would not have supplied.<br><br><b>The Target's Community and Bystanders:</b><br><b>A1010.002: Subject Disengages from Community.</b> Community loss is another harm Cai and colleagues identify, and it falls on the people who were in the room rather than on the person who was targeted.<br><b>A1009.002: Degrade Quality of Information Ecosystem.</b> [Kumar and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03697) find that conflicts reduce the overall activity of users in targeted communities over the long term.<br><br><b>Wider Audience:</b><br><b>A1010.004: Subject Declines to Start Sharing Information.</b> A mob is a public event by construction, and the people watching draw the intended conclusion.<br><b>A1007.002: Activity Dissuades Action.</b> Deterring bystanders is usually the point of a demonstrative pile-on rather than a side effect of it.<br><b>A1009.001: Spread Hate.</b> Abusive and discriminatory material delivered into a shared space reaches everyone present.
