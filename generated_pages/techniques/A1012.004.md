# Assessment A1012.004: Subject Self-Harms

**Summary**: Summary: Activity has the assessed impact of contributing to the subject inflicting harm on themselves.<br><br>Detail: This impact records that the subject harmed themselves, and that an analyst assesses the activity to have contributed to that outcome. This definition specifies "contributory" rather than "causal", because self-harm has multiple causes and an assessment claiming sole causation would rarely be defensible.<br><br>Two types of material can induce self-harming behaviour. In the first, content promoting or depicting self-harm reaches a subject who is already vulnerable, and the way a platform selects and serves that content is part of the mechanism rather than incidental to it. A subject who never sought such material can be shown a great deal of it. In the second, the subject harms themselves in response to being targeted. This is documented as a consequence of sustained harassment, of doxxing, and of sextortion, and in those cases the content in question is about the subject rather than about self-harm.<br><br>Example<br><br>The inquest into the death of Molly Russell, a fourteen-year-old who died in 2017, concluded in September 2022 that [she died from an act of self-harm while suffering depression and the negative effects of online content.](https://mollyrosefoundation.org/mollys-inquest/) The coroner, Andrew Walker, found that [the material she had viewed affected her mental health in a negative way and contributed to her death in a more than minimal way.](https://mollyrosefoundation.org/mollys-inquest/) The inquest heard that [of the 16,300 posts Molly saved, shared, or liked on Instagram in the six months before she died, 2,100 related to depression, self-harm, and suicide, and that the platform's algorithms had at times pushed this content to her without her having requested it.](https://mollyrosefoundation.org/mollys-inquest/) Executives from Meta and Pinterest gave evidence at the inquest. [Meta's head of health and wellbeing, Elizabeth Lagone, apologised after admitting that some of the content Molly viewed had breached the platform's own guidelines, and the Russell family's lawyer said afterwards that Pinterest had engaged with the inquest more fully than Meta had.](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-guardian-usa/20221001/282179359966646)
