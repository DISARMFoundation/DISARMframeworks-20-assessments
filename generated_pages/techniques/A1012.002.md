# Assessment A1012.002: Subject Subjected to Physical Sexual Violence

**Summary**: Summary: Activity has the assessed impact of causing the subject to be subjected to physical sexual violence.<br><br>Detail: This impact records that the subject was subjected to physical sexual violence which an analyst assesses to be connected to the activity.<br><br>Physical sexual violence can be assessed as an impact of an operation where the actor and the person who conducts the violence are different, the normal pattern in influence operations. An operation that publishes a target's home address does not itself commit physical sexual violence; assessing a resulting attack as an impact of the publication is an analytical claim which can be made.<br><br>Physical sexual violence does not require an identified assailant. Where a subject is attacked by an unidentified assailant, the impact is assessable even though no individual actor can be named.<br><br>Example<br><br>In December 2009 a woman in Casper, [Wyoming was raped at knifepoint in her home by Ty Oliver McDowell, a stranger. McDowell had answered a Craigslist advertisement placed by her former partner, Jebidiah James Stipe, who had posed as her, stated that she wished to act out a rape fantasy, and supplied her details.](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2010-jan-11-la-na-rape-craigslist11-2010jan11-story.html) McDowell said he had been duped into committing the offence.<br><br>Between October 2016 and March 2017 [Matthew Herrick was located by more than 1,000 men who arrived at his home and workplace expecting sex, at times sixteen in a day](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-grindr-app/grindr-defeats-appeal-over-harassment-on-gay-dating-app-idUSKCN1R81WD/). His former partner had created Grindr profiles using Herrick's photographs, falsely stating he was seeking unprotected sex and instructing men to treat any resistance from him as part of an agreed rape fantasy. [Herrick submitted around 100 reports of impersonation to Grindr and received automated responses](https://epic.org/documents/herrick-v-grindr-llc/). A federal appeals court subsequently held that [Grindr was not liable under Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/herrick-v-grindr-why-section-230-communications-decency-act-must-be-fixed).<br><br>This case demonstrates an attempt at bringing about physical sexual violence using impersonation, and platforms’ failure to adequately address this.<br><br>The Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on Myanmar documented [mass sexual violence against Rohingya women and girls during the 2017 clearance operations, including public gang rape, with many survivors subsequently killed or mutilated](https://www.ohchr.org/EN/NewsEvents/Pages/DisplayNews.aspx?NewsID=24907&LangID=E). The Mission also found that [Facebook had been a "useful instrument" for those seeking to spread hate, in a context where, for most users, Facebook was the internet](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/HRBodies/HRCouncil/FFM-Myanmar/A_HRC_39_64.pdf). Meta later commissioned a human rights impact assessment which accepted that the company had not done enough to prevent the spread of hatred against the Rohingya on the platform.<br><br>This case demonstrates an indirect pathway, in which individual acts of violence are not directly linked to single pieces of content, so the assessment concerns the conditions the activity created rather than causation of a specific act.
