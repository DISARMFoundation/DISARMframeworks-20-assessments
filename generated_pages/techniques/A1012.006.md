# Assessment A1012.006: Subject Killed

**Summary**: Summary: The subject has been killed by another person in connection with or as a result of activity.<br><br>Detail: This impact records that a person killed the subject, and that an analyst assesses a connection to the activity.<br><br>Sustained campaigns which discredit, dehumanise, or isolate a target frequently precede physical action against them. These campaigns identify the target as legitimate to those who might act, and they degrade the target's standing enough that the response to the killing is muted. An assessment connecting an information operation to a killing claims a contribution to those conditions. It does not assert that the operation carried out the killing.<br><br>The person who kills the subject is often not the actor running the operation and may have had no contact with them. That does not prevent the assessment, but it determines what evidence is needed to support it.<br><br>Killings of this kind are frequently preceded by a long documented record of threat and harassment which was reported at the time and not acted on. That record is usually the strongest available evidence of the connection.<br><br>Example<br><br>In India false child-abduction warnings circulating on WhatsApp produced mob attacks on strangers, leading to death (A1012.006: Subject Killed) and injury (A1012.001: Subject Physically Injured). [More than two dozen people were beaten to death across India between late April and mid-July 2018](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2018/7/17/in-india-whatsapp-stirs-up-deadly-rumours) on suspicion of child abduction. In Rainpada, in Maharashtra, [five nomadic men were bludgeoned to death after one was seen speaking to a child at a weekly market, in a village that had been abuzz for days with WhatsApp rumours of a kidnapping gang](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/india-lynching-attacks-child-abduction-gang-rumors-arrests-maharashtra/). In Bidar, in Karnataka, a mob killed one man and wounded three others after photographs of them were circulated on WhatsApp to alert neighbouring villages, and [four police officers were injured when they tried to stop the attack](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/the-scotsman/20180716/281917363853192).
