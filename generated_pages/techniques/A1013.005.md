# Assessment A1013.005: Subject's Associates Distance Themselves from Subject

**Summary**: Summary: Activity has the assessed impact of causing people connected to the subject to reduce or end that connection.<br><br>Detail: This impact records the subject's personal and professional contacts withdrawing. It covers friends, colleagues, collaborators, sources, neighbours, and acquaintances, and it does not require that any relationship formally ended.<br><br>There are a variety of ways an incident may lead associates of a subject to distance themselves. Associates can withdraw to protect themselves, having concluded that association carries consequences they are unwilling to bear. Associates can accept claims made about the subject in an incident. Associates withdraw because association has become costly, and without their believing anything, as being publicly linked to a person under sustained attack can carry a reputational price of its own.<br><br>Associates can also withdraw because the subject of activity has changed. Where activity has drawn the subject into a movement, a conspiracy, or a set of beliefs their circle does not share, the relationships end because the subject is no longer someone their contacts recognise.<br><br>Example<br><br>Wandrea "Shaye" Moss had worked in elections in Fulton County, Georgia for ten years when Rudy Giuliani made false public claims that she and her mother, Ruby Freeman, had committed electoral fraud during the 2020 presidential election. The claims produced [sustained racist and violent harassment, including death threats;](https://www.npr.org/2022/06/22/1106459556/shaye-moss-staffed-an-election-office-in-georgia-then-she-was-targeted-by-trump) Moss told congressional investigators the harassment made her afraid to leave her home or use her name in public, and she eventually left her job with the county altogether. Freeman testified at trial that she had [lost her name and her reputation, eventually sold her home, and lived out of her car for a time, as the harassment made friends and fellow church members afraid to be associated with her.](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/georgia-election-worker-tearfully-describes-fleeing-her-home-after-giulianis-false-claims-of-fraud)<br><br>This case demonstrates people with a relationship to the subject withdrawing their association with the subject out of fear of association. Freeman's testimony attributes the withdrawal to fear of being associated with her, which places it on the risk of association rather than on belief in the allegation.
